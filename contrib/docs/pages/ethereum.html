Ethereum, USDT and USDC in Biscuit

Ethereum is optional in Biscuit, like every coin besides Monero. USDT and USDC, the two dollar tokens, are added on top of it. Each of them can be held on two networks: Ethereum itself, and Arbitrum, where fees cost a fraction of a cent.

Adding them

Tick them when you create your wallet, or add them later with the + next to the coins on Send and Receive. Each is added on its own and gets its own tab: ETH, USDT and USDC on Ethereum, ETH (Arbitrum), USDT (Arbitrum) and USDC (Arbitrum) on Arbitrum. Adding a token adds the ETH of its network too, because its network fees are paid in that ETH.

Ethereum or Arbitrum

Arbitrum is a network built on top of Ethereum. The coins are the same, but a send there costs a fraction of a cent instead of a few cents or more. Most exchanges let you withdraw ETH, USDT and USDC on Arbitrum: choose Arbitrum One as the network when you withdraw.

EthereumETH, USDT, USDCA few cents, much more when the network is busy

Your address is the same on both networks, but the balances are separate: ETH sent on Arbitrum shows on the Arbitrum tab, not on the Ethereum one. Each receive page says which network it is for. On Arbitrum, Biscuit holds Tether’s USDT and Circle’s native USDC, not the older bridged USDC.e.

Seed and address

Your first Ethereum wallet comes from the same 12 words as your other coins, unless you gave it its own words when adding it. Biscuit uses the address most Ethereum wallets use for these words, so they can be restored elsewhere:

CoinAddressPath
Ethereum, USDT, USDC, on both networks0x…m/44’/60’/0’/0/0

One address receives everything. Unlike Bitcoin, it doesn’t change after a payment: anyone who knows it can see what it received on a block explorer.

Other networks

USDT and USDC exist on many more networks. Biscuit holds them on Ethereum and Arbitrum only. USDT or USDC sent to your address on another network doesn’t show in Biscuit. From Tron, whose addresses start with T, it can’t even reach a 0x address. On networks that use 0x addresses, such as BNB Chain, Polygon or Base, your 12 words still control it: restore them in a wallet that supports that network to get it back. Sent to an exchange on a network it doesn’t support, it is usually lost. Always tell the sender which of the two networks to use. Biscuit says so on the receive page, and swaps only offer the versions Biscuit holds.

Biscuit only handles the real USDT and USDC contracts. Tokens with the same name sent to your address by strangers are ignored.

Several wallets

As with the other coins, you can keep several Ethereum wallets in the same Biscuit wallet: Add wallet, above the address, creates one or restores one from its 12 or 24 words, for example the seed of a wallet you used elsewhere. Switch with the buttons above the address; right-click one to rename it, show its seed or remove it. Back up the seed of each wallet you add.

Frequently asked questions

Does Biscuit hold my coins?
No. Your keys are created on your computer and stay there. There’s no account and no server of ours that could hold or move your coins.
Is Biscuit free?
Yes, and it doesn’t add any fee to your transactions. When you use an exchange swap, we may get a share of the exchange’s fee. You pay the same as you would on the exchange, and it never changes how offers are ranked.
How is Biscuit related to Feather Wallet?
Biscuit is built on Feather. Monero works exactly as it does in Feather, and we added more cryptos and swaps on top. The Feather developers aren’t involved in Biscuit, so please send Biscuit problems to us, not to them. Which one suits you? See Feather Wallet or Biscuit.
Can I use my Biscuit seed in another wallet?
Yes. The Monero seed is a standard Polyseed. The seed of your other coins is standard BIP39: native SegWit paths (BIP84) for Bitcoin and Litecoin, the usual BIP44 path for Dogecoin, the usual Ethereum path, and the Solana path Phantom and Solflare use.
Why does my Bitcoin address change after I get paid?
So the people who pay you can’t see your other payments on a block explorer. The old addresses keep working. Find them under All addresses… on Receive, or tick Keep this address after payments if you want one that never changes. See Addresses.
Which exchange swaps work in Tor mode?
Only Biscuit’s own exchanges: PegasusSwap, ETZ-Swap and Stereoswap, which never ask for identity and accept Tor. Biscuit reaches them through its onion address, so the swap never leaves Tor. Trocador is off in Tor mode, because it requires us to record the IP address each swap is created from. Atomic swaps work over Tor too. See Exchange swaps, Atomic swaps and the privacy policy.
Why does Windows or macOS warn me before opening Biscuit?
Biscuit isn’t signed with a Microsoft certificate or notarized by Apple, so both systems are cautious the first time. Verify the file, then open it as described for Windows and macOS.
My antivirus flags Biscuit as a coin miner.
Monero’s code includes its mining algorithm, and some antivirus software flags that on sight. Biscuit never mines. Verify your download to make sure it’s ours.
I lost my password.
Nobody can recover it, us included. Restore your wallet from your seeds with New/Restore and pick a new password.
Where do I report a bug?
Help › Report bug, or write to us from the contact page. Never send your seed or your wallet files to anyone.
Is Biscuit open source?
Yes. Biscuit is free software under the BSD 3-Clause License, like Feather and Monero, and its atomic swap helper is under the GPL-3.0. The source of every release is on the download page, and releases can be rebuilt from it.
Is there a phone version?
No. Biscuit is a desktop wallet for Windows, macOS and Linux. Your seeds work in phone wallets too, if you need your coins on the go.
Which coins does Biscuit support?
Monero, Bitcoin, Litecoin, Dogecoin, Ethereum and Solana, with USDT and USDC on Ethereum and Arbitrum. Monero works on its own; the others are added only if you want them. We’d rather do a few coins well than many badly.
Why do I need ETH to send USDT or USDC?
Because on Ethereum every transaction pays its network fee in ETH, whatever it sends. USDT and USDC are balances kept by a contract on Ethereum, so moving them is an Ethereum transaction. Keep a few dollars of ETH on the same address: a send usually costs a few cents. Receiving costs nothing. See Why do I need ETH to send USDT?
Can I move my wallet to another computer?
Yes: copy your wallet files (see Wallet files and folders) and open them there, or restore from your seeds. Copying the files keeps your labels, contacts and swap history.

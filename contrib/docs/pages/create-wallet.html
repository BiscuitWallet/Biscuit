Create a wallet

A Biscuit wallet holds Monero, and the other coins you choose, under one password. Creating one takes a few minutes, most of it writing your seeds on paper.

Step by step

  1. In the menu, choose Create new wallet. If a wallet is already open, use File › New/Restore.
  2. Your Monero seed: 16 words (a Polyseed). Write them on paper, in order.
  3. Other coins: tick the ones you want in this wallet, among Bitcoin, Litecoin, Ethereum, USDT and USDC, or none. Monero works on its own, and you can add any coin later.
  4. If you ticked a coin, its seed: 12 words (BIP39), one seed for all the coins you ticked. Write them down too, next to the Monero seed, and tick I have written down these 12 words. Biscuit then asks you to type three of them from your paper, to make sure your copy is right.
  5. Give the wallet a name. Leave the folder as it is, unless you have a reason to change it.
  6. Choose a password. It encrypts the wallet on this computer; a password manager such as KeePassXC can make a strong one for you.

The wallet opens. Before you receive any money, read Seeds and backups.

Adding coins later

The + next to the coins, on the Send and Receive tabs, adds Bitcoin, Litecoin, Ethereum, USDT or USDC at any time. The first coin you add gets a new seed, or one you restore; the next ones use the same 12 words, so your paper already covers them. USDT and USDC run on Ethereum: adding one adds Ethereum too. See One wallet, several coins.

Why two seeds?

Monero and the other coins derive their keys in different ways, and each has its own standard. Keeping to the standards means your seeds also work in other wallets: the Monero seed in Feather, Cake Wallet or Monero’s own wallet, the 12 words in Sparrow, Electrum, MetaMask or any BIP39 wallet. Your password, on the other hand, is one for everything.

Options

  • Options › Extend this seed with a passphrase, on the Monero seed page, adds a passphrase to your Monero seed. You’ll need both to restore it. This is not your wallet password: if you don’t know what it is for, leave it.
  • Ctrl+K on the Monero seed page opens a tool that adds dice rolls or coin flips to the randomness of your seed. See Monero seeds.

One wallet, several coins

A Biscuit wallet isn’t one wallet per coin. It’s one wallet that holds Monero, and the other coins you add, behind a single password. Send, Receive and History work for every coin: pick it at the top.

What’s inside a wallet

  • One Monero wallet, with as many accounts and subaddresses as you like. To keep completely separate Monero wallets, create several Biscuit wallets.
  • The coins you add, if any: Bitcoin, Litecoin and Ethereum, each with one or more wallets, and USDT and USDC, which live on your Ethereum wallets. The first wallets of these coins share a 12-word seed; each wallet you add has its own. See Bitcoin and Litecoin and Ethereum, USDT and USDC.

Only the coins you added show in the tabs, the status bar and the prices on Home.

Adding and removing coins

  • +, next to the coins on Send and Receive, adds a coin. The first one gets a new or restored seed; the next ones use the same words, and Biscuit offers to show them so you can check your paper. Use other words… gives a coin its own seed instead, new or restored, kept apart from the others.
  • USDT and USDC run on Ethereum, and their network fees are paid in ETH: adding one adds Ethereum too.
  • Right-click a wallet button, above the address, to remove it. When a coin has no wallet left, it leaves the tabs. Removing the last one of all erases the file of these seeds, as if you had never added a coin.

Removing a wallet removes it from Biscuit only: its coins stay on the blockchain, and its seed brings them back at any time.

On your disk, that’s a few files with the wallet’s name, all encrypted with your password: see Wallet files and folders.

The tabs

Home
Prices, a currency converter, the Journal, Monero’s crowdfunding proposals. See The Home tab.
History, Send, Receive
For every coin: pick it at the top. + adds one.
Swap
Exchange swaps and atomic swaps, see Swaps.
Coins, Contacts, Notes
Hidden until you turn them on in the View menu. Coins is coin control (Monero, Bitcoin, Litecoin), Contacts your address book, Notes a place for your own notes.

The status bar

On the left of the bottom bar, the sync status. On the right, what you hold: each coin with a balance, under its own name, and the total in your currency (click them for the details, see Balance). Then a row of buttons:

  • the connection status of the Monero node: connected, connecting, behind or disconnected;
  • the Monero account switcher;
  • the lock, to change your password;
  • the settings, and the light or dark theme;
  • your seeds;
  • the onion, purple in Tor mode, grey for a direct connection. Click it to change.

When an update is out, a button for it appears there too.

Several wallets

File › Open wallet opens another wallet; File › Recently open lists the last ones. File › Close wallet goes back to the menu. Your Bitcoin, Litecoin and Ethereum wallets switch from the buttons above the address on Send and Receive; right-click one to rename it, show its seed or remove it.
